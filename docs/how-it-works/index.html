Four steps.

  1. 01

    Tell us about your home.

    You

    Your address and a few questions. Upload your current declarations page or non-renewal notice if you have one; a phone photo works. If you have a closing date or policy end date, tell us and we'll plan around it.

  2. 02

    We build your application.

    Software, reviewed by your broker

    Our software pulls property records, wildfire hazard data, and aerial imagery, then guides you through a short photo walkthrough of the home. It prepares a complete, insurer-ready application. A licensed broker reviews every one before it reaches an insurer.

  3. 03

    We shop every market.

    Your broker

    We match your home to the insurers likely to quote it: private client and other standard insurers, the FAIR Plan with a companion policy, and surplus lines insurers. Before placing a home with a surplus lines insurer, we complete the search of licensed insurers that California requires.

  4. 04

    You choose, with a plan.

    You and your broker

    Your broker compares your options side by side in plain English, flags limits and exclusions, and recommends what fits. You decide. Then you receive your insurability report and a hardening plan to make your home easier to insure next year.

The coverage we shop

Three kinds of coverage, in plain English.

Standard homeowners policy Admitted

Full coverage from an insurer licensed by California, often including private client insurers that specialize in high-value homes. Rates are regulated, policies are backed by the state's insurance guarantee fund, and they're eligible for California's wildfire mitigation discounts. The best outcome when an insurer will write the home, often after hardening.

FAIR Plan plus a companion policy Two policies

A fire policy from the FAIR Plan, California's insurer of last resort, paired with a companion policy (called a DIC, or difference in conditions, policy) that adds theft, water damage, liability, and the rest. For homes standard insurers won't write today.

Surplus lines homeowners policy E&S

Full coverage from an insurer not licensed by California but allowed to cover homes licensed insurers decline. More flexible, often more expensive. These insurers are not backed by California's insurance guarantee fund if they fail. You'll sign a state notice (form D-1) before placement and have five days to cancel after receiving it.

Before you start

What to have ready.

Don't have everything? Start anyway. Your broker will help fill the gaps.

  • The property address
  • Your current declarations page, if you have one
  • Your non-renewal notice, if you received one
  • Your closing date or policy end date
  • Recent upgrades (roof, vents, cleared 5-foot zone), with dates or receipts if you have them
