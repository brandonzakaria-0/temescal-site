What insurers look at

Five features that shape your options.

Hardening means making a home more resistant to embers and fire. These are the features insurers check first.

  1. 1Roof. A Class A fire-rated roof is one of the first things insurers ask about.
  2. 2Vents. Ember-resistant vents help keep embers out of the attic and crawlspace.
  3. 3The first five feet. An ember-resistant zone around the house, kept free of anything that can catch.
  4. 4Defensible space. Cleared or managed vegetation beyond that zone that slows a fire's approach.
  5. 5Hazard zone. The state's fire hazard rating for your area. You can't change it, but it shapes which markets are open.
Zone 0

The first five feet matter most.

The ember-resistant zone is the strip within five feet of your home. Keeping it clear of wood mulch, dense plants, firewood, and anything else that can catch gives embers nothing to land on.

It's often one of the most practical places to start, and your hardening plan will say exactly what to change on your property.

It's also becoming the rule. In August 2026, California's Board of Forestry approved "Zone 0" rules for the five feet around homes in the state's highest fire hazard areas. New construction comes first, and existing homes are phased in over several years. Getting ahead of it now helps with insurers too.

Programs insurers recognize

Hardening that counts.

Safer from Wildfires

California's program defining the mitigation steps that licensed insurers must recognize with discounts: a Class A roof, ember-resistant vents, enclosed eaves, upgraded windows, six inches of noncombustible material at the base of the walls, the five-foot ember-resistant zone, a clear space under decks, sheds moved at least 30 feet away, defensible space, and community programs like Firewise USA. See the state's list.

IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home

A designation from the Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety that verifies a home meets wildfire hardening standards.

Discounts for mitigation vary widely by insurer and home, from a few percent to more than 40%, according to research from Resources for the Future. Your broker will tell you which apply to you.
