The FAIR Plan, explained

What it covers, and what it doesn't.

The California FAIR Plan is the state's insurer of last resort, for homes standard insurers won't cover. It mainly covers fire. Most households pair it with a second policy to fill the gaps.

FAIR Plan policy Fire, smoke, lightning, and explosions inside the home Dwelling coverage tops out at $3 million, below the rebuild value of many high-value homes.
+
Companion policy (DIC) Theft, water damage, liability, and the rest "Difference in conditions" insurance, written by a separate insurer to fill the FAIR Plan's gaps.
=
Together Fuller protection, with two policies and two bills Your broker coordinates both, and checks the combined limits against your home's rebuild value.
Rebuild value

Is your home underinsured?

Rebuild value, also called replacement cost, is what it would cost to rebuild your home today at its current size and quality. For custom homes in the hills and on the coast, it can be well above what a FAIR Plan policy can pay.

Your coverage review starts there: your broker estimates the gap, then looks for coverage that closes it, whether that's added coverage alongside the FAIR Plan, a surplus lines policy, or a return to a standard insurer.

A path off the FAIR Plan

Hardening can reopen standard options.

Hardening means making a home more resistant to embers and fire. Insurers look closely at a few features, and improving them can make a home eligible for more insurers. California also requires licensed insurers to offer discounts for qualifying mitigation.

Roof

A Class A fire-rated roof.

Vents

Ember-resistant vents that keep embers out of the attic and crawlspace.

The first five feet

An ember-resistant zone around the house, clear of anything that can catch.

Defensible space

Managed vegetation beyond that zone that slows a fire's approach.
