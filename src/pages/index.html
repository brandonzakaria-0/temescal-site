The wildfire checklist

The upgrades insurers look for in wildfire country.

California's Safer from Wildfires program lists the hardening steps licensed insurers must recognize with discounts. We check your home against each one, tell you which matter most for your home, and help you document the work so it counts.

  1. 01

    Class A roof

    One of the first things an insurer asks about. Most tile, composition, and metal roofs qualify.

  2. 02

    Ember-resistant vents

    Fine metal mesh keeps wind-blown embers out of the attic and crawlspace.

  3. 03

    The first five feet

    No mulch, firewood, or dense plants against the house, so embers have nothing to land on.

  4. 04

    Six inches at the base

    Noncombustible material at the bottom of exterior walls, where embers pile up.

  5. 05

    Enclosed eaves

    Closed-in eaves keep heat and embers from collecting under the roofline.

  6. 06

    Upgraded windows

    Multi-pane glass or shutters hold up better against radiant heat.

  7. 07

    Clear under decks

    No plants, leaves, or stored items under a deck attached to the house.

  8. 08

    Defensible space

    Managed vegetation around the home, with sheds and outbuildings at least 30 feet away.

  9. 09

    Community programs

    Firewise USA sites and Fire Risk Reduction Communities can add a community discount.

Discounts vary by insurer and home, from a few percent to more than 40%, according to Resources for the Future. Surplus lines insurers aren't bound by the program, and no upgrade guarantees coverage, but each one gives insurers one less reason to say no.

Every market, side by side

Three ways to insure a home in California. We compare all of them.

Insurers issue the policies. Your broker works for you, shops each market that might cover your home, and explains the tradeoffs before you decide.

Standard (admitted)

Standard homeowners policy

Full coverage from an insurer licensed by California, including private client insurers that specialize in high-value homes.

When it fits
The preferred option when an insurer will write the home, often after hardening.
Good to know
Eligible for California's wildfire mitigation discounts, and backed by the state's insurance guarantee fund.
Two policies together

FAIR Plan plus a companion policy

A fire policy from the FAIR Plan, California's insurer of last resort, paired with a companion policy (called DIC) that fills the gaps.

When it fits
Homes standard insurers won't write today.
Good to know
Two policies, two bills. FAIR Plan limits can fall short of a high-value home's rebuild value.
Surplus lines (E&S)

Surplus lines homeowners policy

Full coverage from an insurer not licensed by California but allowed to cover homes licensed insurers decline. More flexible, often more expensive.

When it fits
Higher-value or hard-to-place homes, or when the FAIR Plan pairing falls short.
Good to know
Not backed by California's insurance guarantee fund if the insurer fails. You sign a state notice (form D-1) before placement.

Questions

What people ask first.

All questions
Are you an insurance company?

No. We're an independent, licensed brokerage. We work for you and shop many insurers to find coverage that fits.

Will hardening my home lower my premium?

It can. California requires licensed insurers to offer discounts for the hardening steps in its Safer from Wildfires program, and some insurers won't consider a home until certain upgrades are done. Amounts vary by insurer, so your broker will tell you which discounts apply to your home.

Do you do the hardening work?

No. We're an insurance brokerage, not a contractor. We show you which upgrades insurers look for, help you decide what to do first, and help you document finished work so it counts with insurers.

Do you use AI? Who looks at my application?

Our software handles the paperwork so we can move fast. A licensed broker reviews every application and every recommendation, and you can always talk to a person.

I got a non-renewal notice. What should I do?

Don't wait until the last week. Send us the notice and your address, and we'll start looking at every option while you still have time.

What is the FAIR Plan?

California's insurer of last resort. It mainly covers fire, so most homeowners pair it with a companion policy (called a DIC policy) for theft, water damage, liability, and more.
