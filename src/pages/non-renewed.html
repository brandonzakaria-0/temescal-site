The basics

What a non-renewal means.

Your insurer has decided not to renew your policy when its current term ends. It is not a judgment about you, and many homeowners in wildfire country have received one as insurers pull back.

Your coverage stays in force until the end date on the notice. That date is your deadline. The earlier you start, the more options you keep.

Your options

Most homes have more than one path.

Your broker compares them side by side and explains the tradeoffs before you decide.

Standard (admitted)

Another licensed insurer

Including private client insurers that specialize in high-value homes. Often possible after hardening.

Two policies

FAIR Plan plus a companion policy

A fire policy from California's insurer of last resort, paired with a companion (DIC) policy for theft, water damage, liability, and more.

Surplus lines (E&S)

A surplus lines policy

Full coverage from an insurer not licensed by California. Flexible, often more expensive, and not backed by the state guarantee fund.

Timing

Why to start early.

Some insurers need photos or an inspection before they quote. Hardening steps take time to schedule. And before a home can be placed with a surplus lines insurer, California requires a diligent search of licensed insurers first.

Starting early leaves room for all of it, so you choose from the best options rather than the last one available.
